1. Normal & Shear Stress on an Inclined Plane

⏱ 45 min 🏆 5 marks 🔗 Prereq: None

📖 Exam Definitions — quote these in your answers

  • Normal stress: intensity of force acting perpendicular to a plane, sigma = P/A.
  • Shear stress: intensity of force parallel to a plane, tau.
  • Hooke's law: within the elastic limit stress is proportional to strain; modulus E = sigma/e.

📝 Theory Notes

Given the plane-stress state (σₓ, σᵧ, τₓᵧ) on x–y faces, find the stresses on a plane whose normal makes angle θ with the x-axis:

$$\sigma_\theta = (\sigma_{x} + \sigmaᵧ)/2 + (\sigma_{x} - \sigmaᵧ)/2 \cdot \cos 2\theta + \tau_{x}ᵧ \cdot \sin 2\theta\\\tau_\theta = (\sigma_{x} - \sigmaᵧ)/2 \cdot \sin 2\theta - \tau_{x}ᵧ \cdot \cos 2\theta$$
  • Derivation: take a wedge element, apply force equilibrium in the normal and tangential directions, divide by area.
  • Sign convention: tensile σ positive; τ positive when it tends to rotate the element anticlockwise; θ measured anticlockwise from x-axis.
Most repeated SOM question. Derivation asked in MO2023 END Q.1(a); numerical asked in MO2024 P2 Q.1(a) and MO2025 MID Q.1(b) (glue joint).

📘 Worked Example

MO2025 MID Q.1(b): Two wooden joists 50 mm × 100 mm glued along AB. P = 200 kN. The glue joint is at 60° to the load axis. Find normal and shear stress in the glue.

A_AB = 50 × 100 / sin 60° ... (area of the inclined interface) σ = (P/A) sin²θ ; τ = (P/A) sinθ cosθ [uniaxial state, θ = angle with load] P/A = 200×10³ / (50×100×10⁻⁶) = 40 MPa σ_glue = 40 sin²60° = 30 MPa τ_glue = 40 sin60° cos60° = 17.32 MPa

2. Principal Stresses, Planes & Mohr's Circle

⏱ 60 min 🏆 5 marks 🔗 Prereq: Topic 1

📖 Exam Definitions — quote these in your answers

  • Principal planes: planes carrying zero shear stress; normal stresses on them are principal stresses (max/min).
  • Mohr's circle: graphical transformation of stress — every plane's (sigma, tau) lies on a circle of diameter sigma1-sigma2.
  • Strain rosette: three strain gauges at known angles used to compute principal strains.

📝 Theory Notes

Principal stresses: σ₁,₂ = (σₓ + σᵧ)/2 ± √[ ((σₓ − σᵧ)/2)² + τₓᵧ² ] Orientation of principal planes: tan 2θₚ = 2τₓᵧ / (σₓ − σᵧ) Maximum shear stress: τ_max = √[ ((σₓ − σᵧ)/2)² + τₓᵧ² ] = (σ₁ − σ₂)/2
  • On principal planes τ = 0.
  • σ₁ = max principal (most tensile), σ₂ = min principal (most compressive).
  • Planes of max shear are 45° from principal planes; on them σ_avg = (σ₁+σ₂)/2.
  • Mohr's circle (Pytel convention): centre C = ((σₓ+σᵧ)/2, 0), radius R = τ_max. Shear τ is positive when it produces a clockwise moment about the element. Plot point X = (σₓ, τₓᵧ) and point Y = (σᵧ, −τₓᵧ) (both shear signs per the clockwise rule); σ₁, σ₂ are the intersections with the σ-axis. Rotate 2θ in the same direction as the plane rotation.
Repeated type: "Given σₓ, σᵧ, τₓᵧ, determine orientation of principal planes and values of principal stresses" — MO2023 END Q.1(b), MO2024 P2 Q.1(b), MO2025 MID Q.2.

📘 Worked Example

MO2023 END Q.1(b): σₓ = 50 MPa, σᵧ = −10 MPa, τₓᵧ = 40 MPa.

σ_avg = (50 − 10)/2 = 20 MPa R = √[((50+10)/2)² + 40²] = √[30² + 40²] = 50 MPa σ₁ = 20 + 50 = 70 MPa ; σ₂ = 20 − 50 = −30 MPa tan 2θₚ = 2×40 / (50 − (−10)) = 80/60 = 1.333 → 2θₚ = 53.13° → θₚ = 26.57° τ_max = 50 MPa

MO2025 END Q.1(a): σ₁ = 20 MPa, σ₂ = −80 MPa known → find σₓ, σᵧ on a given element orientation:

If element is at 45° to principal axes: σₓ = (σ₁+σ₂)/2 + (σ₁−σ₂)/2 cos 90° = −30 MPa σᵧ = (σ₁+σ₂)/2 − (σ₁−σ₂)/2 cos 90° = −30 MPa τₓᵧ = −(σ₁−σ₂)/2 sin 90° = −50 MPa

3. Generalized Hooke's Law & Strain Analysis

⏱ 45 min 🏆 5 marks 🔗 Prereq: Topic 2

📖 Exam Definitions — quote these in your answers

  • Generalized Hooke's law: relations linking all six stresses and strains through E, G and K.
  • Volumetric strain: change of volume per unit volume; E = 2G(1+nu) = 3K(1-2nu).

📝 Theory Notes

εₓ = (σₓ − νσᵧ)/E (plane stress) εᵧ = (σᵧ − νσₓ)/E γₓᵧ = τₓᵧ / G , G = E / [2(1+ν)]

Solving for stresses from measured strains (strain rosette / gauge data):

$$\sigma_{x} = E(\epsilon_{x} + \nu\epsilonᵧ)/(1 - \nu^{2})\\\sigmaᵧ = E(\epsilonᵧ + \nu\epsilon_{x})/(1 - \nu^{2})\\\tau_{x}ᵧ = G \gamma_{x}ᵧ$$
MO2025 END Q.1(b): εₓ = −400×10⁻⁶, εᵧ = 200×10⁻⁶, γₓᵧ = 800×10⁻⁶, E = 200 GPa, ν = 0.3 → find principal stresses and max shear. Plug strains into the formulas above, then use Topic 2 formulas on the resulting σₓ, σᵧ, τₓᵧ.

Strain transformation (plane strain)

ε_θ = (εₓ+εᵧ)/2 + (εₓ−εᵧ)/2 · cos 2θ + (γₓᵧ/2)·sin 2θ (γ/2)_θ = −(εₓ−εᵧ)/2 · sin 2θ + (γₓᵧ/2)·cos 2θ Same form as stress transformation with σ→ε and τ→γ/2

Principal strains

ε₁,₂ = (εₓ+εᵧ)/2 ± √[ ((εₓ−εᵧ)/2)² + (γₓᵧ/2)² ] tan 2θₚ = γₓᵧ / (εₓ − εᵧ) γ_max = 2·√[ ((εₓ−εᵧ)/2)² + (γₓᵧ/2)² ] = ε₁ − ε₂
  • Principal strains occur on planes where γ = 0.
  • Mohr's circle for strain: centre (εₓ+εᵧ)/2, radius √[(εₓ−εᵧ)/2]² + (γₓᵧ/2)². Plot (εₓ, γₓᵧ/2) and (εᵧ, −γₓᵧ/2).

Strain rosettes (measure ε, solve for σ)

RosetteGauge anglesStrains
Rectangular (45°)0°, 45°, 90°εₓ=ε₀, εᵧ=ε₉₀, γₓᵧ = 2ε₄₅ − ε₀ − ε₉₀
Delta (60°)0°, 60°, 120°γₓᵧ = (2/√3)(ε₆₀ − ε₁₂₀)
Rosette → stresses: plug εₓ, εᵧ, γₓᵧ into the E/(1−ν²) inversion above, then Topic 2 principal-stress formulas.

📘 Worked Example

G = 200×10⁹ / [2(1.3)] = 76.92 GPa σₓ = 200×10⁹ (−400×10⁻⁶ + 0.3×200×10⁻⁶)/(1−0.09) = −74.7 MPa σᵧ = 200×10⁹ (200×10⁻⁶ + 0.3×(−400×10⁻⁶))/(0.91) = 17.6 MPa τₓᵧ = 76.92×10⁹ × 800×10⁻⁶ = 61.5 MPa σ_avg = (−74.7+17.6)/2 = −28.55 ; R = √[46.15² + 61.5²] = 76.9 σ₁ = 48.3 MPa ; σ₂ = −105.4 MPa ; τ_max = 76.9 MPa

4. Shear Force & Bending Moment Diagrams

⏱ 60 min 🏆 2–4 marks 🔗 Prereq: None

📖 Exam Definitions — quote these in your answers

  • Shear force at a section: algebraic sum of transverse forces on one side of the section.
  • Bending moment at a section: algebraic sum of moments of forces on one side about that section.
  • Point of contraflexure: section where bending moment changes sign and equals zero.

📝 Theory Notes

Relationships between load, shear and bending moment (MO2025 END Q.2(a)):

dV/dx = −w(x) (slope of SF diagram = negative load intensity) dM/dx = V(x) (slope of BM diagram = shear force)
  • Shear force at a section: algebraic sum of all transverse forces on one side.
  • Bending moment at a section: algebraic sum of moments of all forces on one side.
  • Sign convention: upward force left of section → +V; sagging moment → +M.
LoadingSF diagramBM diagram
Point loadSudden jump (= P)Linear (slope = V)
U.D.L. wLinear (slope = −w)Parabolic
CoupleNo changeSudden jump (= M)

📘 Worked Example

MO2025 END Q.2(b): Cantilever L = 2 m, U.D.L. w₀ = 50 kN/m. Write V(x), M(x), draw diagrams.

V(x) = −50x kN (x from fixed end, 0 ≤ x ≤ 2) M(x) = −25x² kN·m At x = 2 m: V = −100 kN, M = −100 kN·m (fixed end values) Shape: V linear from 0 → −100 ; M parabolic from 0 → −100

5. Flexure Formula & Bending Stress

⏱ 60 min 🏆 5 marks 🔗 Prereq: Topic 4

📖 Exam Definitions — quote these in your answers

  • Flexure formula: M/I = sigma/y = E/R — bending stress varies linearly across depth.
  • Section modulus Z = I/y_max; sigma_max = M/Z.

📝 Theory Notes

$$M/I = \sigma/y = E/R$$
  • M = bending moment · I = second moment of area about NA · σ = bending stress at distance y from NA · R = radius of curvature
  • σ = M·y/I ; max at the extreme fibres: σ_max = M·y_max/I = M/Z
  • Z = section modulus = I/y_max (rectangle: bd²/6 ; circle: πd³/32)
  • Neutral axis passes through the centroid of the cross-section.
Derivation (MO2025 MID Q.5(a)): assume plane sections remain plane; strain ε = y/R (linear); stress σ = E·y/R; internal moment ∫σ·y dA = EI/R; hence M/I = σ/y = E/R.
MO2025 END Q.2(c): fibre strains measured (AB: +60×10⁻³ mm, CD: −100×10⁻³ mm over 200 mm gauge length, E = 70 GPa) → ε_top = 0.3×10⁻³, ε_bot = −0.5×10⁻³, σ = E·ε → σ_top = 21 MPa (tension), σ_bot = −35 MPa (compression).

📘 Worked Example

MO2025 MID Q.5(b): Band saw 20 mm × 0.8 mm over 600 mm diameter pulleys, E = 200 GPa.

$$y_\max = 0.4 mm ; R = 300 mm\\\sigma_\max = E\cdot y_\max/R = 200\times10^{9} \times 0.4\times10^{-3} / 0.3 = 266.7 MPa$$

6. Shear Stress in Beams

⏱ 45 min 🏆 5 marks 🔗 Prereq: Topic 5

📖 Exam Definitions — quote these in your answers

  • Shear stress distribution in beams: tau = V*A*ybar/(I*b), parabolic for rectangles, maximum at neutral axis.

📝 Theory Notes

$$\tau = V\cdot Aȳ / (I\cdot b)$$
  • V = shear force at section · Aȳ = first moment of area above the level · I = M.I. of whole section · b = width at the level

Rectangular section derivation (asked twice!): parabolic distribution,

τ = (3V/2bd) [1 − 4y²/d²] τ_max at NA (y = 0) = 3V/(2bd) = 1.5 × τ_avg
  • τ = 0 at extreme fibres (y = ±d/2), maximum at the neutral axis.
  • Steps: consider two adjacent sections dx apart; difference in bending stress creates unbalanced force; this is balanced by shear on the horizontal plane → τ = V·Aȳ/(I·b).
Repeated 5-mark derivation: "Derive shear stress in a beam of rectangular cross-section" — MO2023 END Q.2(a) AND MO2024 P2 Q.2(b).

7. Beam Deflection — Macaulay's Method

⏱ 60 min 🏆 5–6 marks 🔗 Prereq: Topic 4–5

📖 Exam Definitions — quote these in your answers

  • Macaulay's method: double integration of EI·y" = M with bracket terms keeping constants common for the whole beam.
  • Moment-area theorems relate slope/deflection changes to areas of the M/EI diagram.

📝 Theory Notes

EI d²y/dx² = M(x)

Macaulay's method: express M(x) in one equation with step brackets ⟨x − a⟩ⁿ which are zero when x < a:

⟨x − a⟩ⁿ = 0 if x < a ; (x − a)ⁿ if x ≥ a Integrate twice: EI dy/dx = ∫M dx + C₁ EI y = ∫∫M dx + C₂ Boundary conditions fix C₁, C₂ (y = 0 at supports, dy/dx = 0 at fixed end).
  • Standard results: cantilever with end load P: δ = PL³/3EI ; cantilever with UDL: δ = wL⁴/8EI ; SS beam central load: δ = PL³/48EI ; SS beam UDL: δ = 5wL⁴/384EI.

Moment area method

First theorem — change in slope between A and B: θ_AB = ∫(M/EI) dx = area of the M/EI diagram between A and B Second theorem — tangential deviation of B from the tangent at A: t_BA = ∫(M/EI)·x dx = first moment of that area about B
  • Sign convention: sagging M/EI gives positive θ and positive t; treat the M/EI diagram areas as concentrated at their centroids when taking first moments.
  • Classic results cross-check: cantilever end load δ = PL³/3EI ; SS beam central load δ = PL³/48EI — both match the Macaulay results above.

📘 Worked Example

MO2023 END Q.3(b): Cantilever length L, point load at L/2 from fixed end. Find deflection at free end.

M(x) = −P⟨x − L/2⟩ (x from fixed end) EI dy/dx = −P⟨x−L/2⟩²/2 + C₁ EI y = −P⟨x−L/2⟩³/6 + C₁x + C₂ At x = 0 (fixed): y = 0, dy/dx = 0 → C₁ = C₂ = 0 Free end x = L: y = −P(L/2)³/6EI = −PL³/48EI δ_free = PL³/48EI (upward sign convention aside)

MO2024 P2 Q.3(a): SS beam with UDL over part — set up M(x) with Macaulay brackets, integrate, use y(0)=y(L)=0, then evaluate at x = L and mid-span.

8. Euler Buckling of Columns

⏱ 45 min 🏆 5 marks 🔗 Prereq: Topic 5

📖 Exam Definitions — quote these in your answers

  • Euler buckling load: P_cr = pi^2*E*I / L_eff^2 — the axial load at which a perfect column buckles elastically.
  • Slenderness ratio: L_eff/r_min; effective length depends on end conditions (0.5L fixed-fixed ... 2L pinned-free).
  • Rankine-Gordon formula: empirical combination of crushing and Euler loads valid for intermediate columns.

📝 Theory Notes

P_cr = π²EI / L_eff²
  • L_eff = effective length (both ends pinned: L ; one fixed one free: 2L ; both fixed: L/2 ; one fixed one pinned: L/√2 ≈ 0.707L)
  • Buckling happens about the axis with the smaller I (weakest direction).
  • Slenderness ratio λ = L_eff / k (k = radius of gyration = √(I/A)).

Derivation: set up EI d²y/dx² = −P·y for a pinned column, solve y = A sin(αx) + B cos(αx) with α² = P/EI; boundary conditions y(0)=0, y(L)=0 give sin(αL)=0 → αL = nπ → P = n²π²EI/L², smallest for n = 1.

Limitations of Euler's formula (MO2025 END Q.4(b), 5 marks): valid only for long/slender columns (λ > critical slenderness); for short columns failure is by crushing (σ = P/A = σ_crushing), not buckling; Euler load assumes perfectly straight, centrally loaded, homogeneous column with pinned ends — real columns have imperfections and eccentricity; valid only while σ_cr < σ_proportional.
MO2024 P2 Q.3(b): 5 m column, four timber pieces (E = 14 GPa) nailed → compute I of the composite section about both axes, take the smaller I, apply P_cr = π²EI/L².

8b. Torsion of Circular Shafts

⏱ 45 min 🏆 5 marks 🔗 Prereq: Topic 5

📖 Exam Definitions — quote these in your answers

  • Torsion equation: T/J = tau/r = G*theta/L for circular shafts in elastic torsion.
  • Polar modulus J/r; power transmitted P = 2*pi*N*T/60.

📝 Theory Notes

Torsion formula: T/J = τ/r = Gθ/L Polar second moment J — solid shaft: J = πd⁴/32 ; hollow shaft: J = π(dₒ⁴ − dᵢ⁴)/32 Power transmitted: P = 2πNT/60 (N in rpm, T in N·m → P in watts) Angle of twist: θ = TL/GJ
  • Strength design: size the shaft so τ_max ≤ τ_allowable ; for a solid shaft τ_max = 16T/πd³.
  • Stiffness design: limit the twist per unit length (e.g. 1°/m): θ/L = T/GJ ≤ allowable rate.
  • Combined bending + torsion on a circular shaft: equivalent torque T_e = √(M² + T²) ; equivalent moment M_e = (M + √(M² + T²))/2.

📘 Worked Example

Solid shaft: d = 50 mm, T = 2 kN·m, G = 80 GPa.

τ = 16T/πd³ = 16×2000/(π×0.05³) = 81.5 MPa θ = TL/GJ → per unit length θ/L = T/GJ = 2000/(80×10⁹ × π×0.05⁴/32)

9. Curved Beams & Shear Center

⏱ 45 min 🏆 5 marks 🔗 Prereq: Topic 5

📖 Exam Definitions — quote these in your answers

  • Winkler-Bach theory: curved-beam stress distribution is hyperbolic; inner fibre stress exceeds simple flexure prediction.
  • Shear centre: point through which a transverse load must act to produce bending without twist.

📝 Theory Notes

Curved beams

For a curved beam (neutral axis shifted towards centre of curvature), the stress distribution is hyperbolic, not linear:

σ = M·y / [A·e·(R_n − y)] where R_n = A / ∫(dA/r)
  • R_n = radius of neutral axis · e = eccentricity = R_c − R_n (R_c = centroidal radius)
  • y = distance from the neutral axis (positive toward the outer fibre; R_n − y = distance from centre of curvature).
  • σ is maximum at the inner fibre (closer to curvature centre).
  • For rectangular section: R_n = h / ln(R_o/R_i).
MO2023 END Q.4(a): "Derive stress distribution due to bending in a curved beam" — standard derivation using ε = y·θ/R_n geometry and ∫σdA = 0, ∫σ·y dA = M.

Shear center of a C-channel (MO2024 P2 Q.4(a))

e = 3b² / (6b + h) [thin-walled channel: b = flange width, h = web depth] Shear flow: q = V·Aȳ/I ; the resultant of flange shears (V·b²·h/4I ...) must pass through the shear centre to avoid twisting.

Strain energy & energy methods

Axial: U = P²L/2AE Bending: U = ∫ M²/(2EI) dx Torsion: U = T²L/2GJ Castigliano's first theorem: δ = ∂U/∂P Unit-load (virtual work) method: δ = ∫ (m·M/EI) dx — m = moment due to a unit load at the point of required deflection

This is the tool used for the MO2025 END Q.4(a) half-ring deflection problem (already referenced in the PYQ table below).

📘 Worked Example

MO2024 P2 Q.4(b): Curved bar, rectangular section 35 mm wide × 70 mm high, inside radius 50 mm, M = 4500 N·m.

R_i = 50, R_o = 120, R_c = 85 mm R_n = 70 / ln(120/50) = 70/0.8755 = 79.95 mm ; e = 85 − 79.95 = 5.05 mm Inner fibre: y_in = R_n − R_i = 29.95 mm ; σ_in = M·y_in/(A·e·R_i) A = 35×70 = 2450 mm² = 2.45×10⁻³ m² σ_in = 4500 × 0.02995 / (2.45×10⁻³ × 0.00505 × 0.05) ≈ 217.8 MPa

10. Thin & Thick Cylinders

⏱ 60 min 🏆 5–6 marks 🔗 Prereq: Topic 1

📖 Exam Definitions — quote these in your answers

  • Thin cylinder hoop stress: sigma_h = p*d/(2t) — circumferential, twice the longitudinal stress.
  • Longitudinal stress: sigma_l = p*d/(4t).
  • Thick cylinder (Lame): radial and hoop stresses vary as A - B/r^2 across the wall.

📝 Theory Notes

Thin cylinder (t < d/20)

Hoop stress: σ_h = p·d / (2t) Longitudinal: σ_l = p·d / (4t) Max shear: τ_max = (σ_h − σ_l)/2 = p·d/(8t) Hoop strain: ε_h = (σ_h − νσ_l)/E = p·d(2 − ν)/(4tE)

Derivation: cut the cylinder along a diametral plane — the pressure force p·d·L is balanced by 2 × σ_h·t·L → σ_h = pd/2t. For longitudinal: p·(πd²/4) = σ_l·πdt → σ_l = pd/4t.

Thick cylinder — Lame's equations (MO2025 END Q.5(a))

Radial: σ_r = A − B/r² Tangential: σ_t = A + B/r² A = (P_i·r_i² − P_o·r_o²)/(r_o² − r_i²) B = r_i²·r_o²·(P_i − P_o)/(r_o² − r_i²)
  • σ_t is maximum at the inner surface (r = r_i) — the critical location.
  • Derivation route: equilibrium of an annular element + compatibility (σ_t − σ_r related to radial displacement), then apply boundary conditions.

Shrink fit (interference fit)

Two cylinders sharing a common interface radius r_p with radial interference δ develop a contact pressure p, found from displacement compatibility via Lame's equations:

p = δ / [ (r_p/E_o)·((r_o² + r_p²)/(r_o² − r_p²) + ν_o) + (r_p/E_i)·((r_p² + r_i²)/(r_p² − r_i²) − ν_i) ]

(book uses E_o, E_i for the outer/inner cylinder moduli). Stresses at any radius then follow from Lame's equations with p acting as external pressure on the inner cylinder and internal pressure on the outer. Note: a rotating disc is NOT covered in Pytel (only the thin ring σ = ρω²r² is available).

📘 Worked Example

MO2023 END Q.5(b): Thick cylinder r_i = 100 mm, r_o = 150 mm, P_i = 60 MPa, P_o = 30 MPa. Find σ_h, σ_r at r = 120 mm.

A = (60×0.01 − 30×0.0225)/(0.0225 − 0.01) = (0.6 − 0.675)/0.0125 = −6 MPa B = 0.01×0.0225×(60−30)/0.0125 = 0.54 MPa·m² At r = 0.12 m: r² = 0.0144 σ_t = −6 + 0.54/0.0144 = 31.5 MPa σ_r = −6 − 0.54/0.0144 = −43.5 MPa

MO2024 P2 Q.5(b): d_i = 200, d_o = 300 mm, σ_y = 430 MPa → max internal pressure (P_o = 0, use Tresca: σ_t(r_i) − σ_r(r_i) = σ_y).

MO2025 END Q.5(b): Tank from 1 mm sheet, p = 500 kPa → σ_h = p·d/2t (need diameter from figure, typically 2 m → σ_h = 500×2/(2×0.001) = 500 MPa... check units) — solve with the same thin-shell formulas.

⚡ Quick Revision Cheat Sheet

Must-Know Formulas by Module

M1 — Stress Transformation & Mohr
Inclined plane ($\theta$ from x-face): $\sigma_\theta=\tfrac{\sigma_x+\sigma_y}{2}+\tfrac{\sigma_x-\sigma_y}{2}\cos2\theta+\tau_{xy}\sin2\theta$ $\tau_\theta=\tfrac{\sigma_x-\sigma_y}{2}\sin2\theta-\tau_{xy}\cos2\theta$
Principal stresses: $\sigma_{1,2}=\tfrac{\sigma_x+\sigma_y}{2}\pm R$, $R=\sqrt{\big(\tfrac{\sigma_x-\sigma_y}{2}\big)^2+\tau_{xy}^2}$ $\tan2\theta_p=\dfrac{2\tau_{xy}}{\sigma_x-\sigma_y}$ · $\tau_{max}=R$
Strain relations: $\epsilon_1=\tfrac{\epsilon_x+\epsilon_y}{2}\pm\sqrt{\big(\tfrac{\epsilon_x-\epsilon_y}{2}\big)^2+\tfrac{\gamma_{xy}^2}{4}}$ Rosette (45°): $\epsilon_x=\epsilon_a,\ \epsilon_y=\epsilon_c,\ \gamma_{xy}=2\epsilon_b-\epsilon_a-\epsilon_c$
M2 — SFD/BMD, Flexure & Shear in Beams
Relations: $w=-\dfrac{dV}{dx}=-\dfrac{d^2M}{dx^2}$ · $V=\dfrac{dM}{dx}$ Max BM where $V=0$. UDL on simple span: $M_{max}=\dfrac{wL^2}{8}$ Point load centre: $M=\dfrac{PL}{4}$
Flexure: $\dfrac{M}{I}=\dfrac{\sigma}{y}=\dfrac{E}{R}$ · $\sigma_{max}=\dfrac{M}{Z}$, $Z=I/y_{max}$ Rect $Z=bh^2/6$ · Circle $Z=\pi d^3/32$
Shear stress: $\tau=\dfrac{VA\bar y}{I b}$ Rectangle: $\tau_{max}=1.5\,\tau_{avg}$ · Circle: $4/3$ Parallel axis: $I=I_G+Ah^2$
M3 — Deflection & Torsion
Cantilever end load: $\delta=\dfrac{PL^3}{3EI}$; UDL tip $\dfrac{wL^4}{8EI}$ SS centre load $\dfrac{PL^3}{48EI}$; UDL $\dfrac{5wL^4}{384EI}$ Macaulay: $EI\,y''=M$ with bracket terms $\langle x-a\rangle^n$
Torsion: $\dfrac{T}{J}=\dfrac{\tau}{r}=\dfrac{G\theta}{L}$, solid $J=\dfrac{\pi d^4}{32}$ Power: $P=\dfrac{2\pi NT}{60}$ · $\tau_{max}$ at surface
M4 — Columns & Energy Methods
Euler: $P_{cr}=\dfrac{\pi^2EI}{L_{eff}^2}$; $L_{eff}$: pinned 1.0L · fixed-free 2L · fixed-fixed 0.5L · fixed-pinned 0.7L Slenderness $=L_{eff}/r$
Rankine: $\dfrac{1}{P_R}=\dfrac{1}{P_C}+\dfrac{1}{P_E}$
Strain energy: axial $U=\dfrac{P^2L}{2AE}$; bending $U=\int\dfrac{M^2}{2EI}dx$; torsion $U=\int\dfrac{T^2}{2GJ}dx$ Castigliano: $\delta=\partial U/\partial P$
M5 — Cylinders
Thin: hoop $\sigma_h=\dfrac{pd}{2t}$ (twice) longitudinal $\sigma_l=\dfrac{pd}{4t}$ Sphere: $\sigma=pd/(4t)$
Thick (Lamé): $\sigma_r=A-\dfrac{B}{r^2}$, $\sigma_\theta=A+\dfrac{B}{r^2}$ Shrink fit produces interfacial pressure → superpose Lamé solutions.
