1. Continuum Concept

⏱ 15 min 📄 Bansal Ch.1 §1.1–1.3 🏆 2 marks 🔗 Prereq: None

📝 Theory Notes

  • Real fluids are molecular, but we treat them as continuum when studying macroscopic behaviour.
  • Assumption valid when characteristic length (e.g. pipe diameter) ≫ mean free path of molecules.
  • Physical properties (ρ, μ, σ) are treated as continuous, NOT discrete.
Exam tip: Always appears as 2-mark L1 question in MID semesters. Asked as "Discuss the concept of continuum with justification" or "What does continuum signify?".

📘 Worked Example

Why does the continuum assumption fail at very low pressures (high vacuum)?

1. At STP, air molecule mean free path ≈ 68 nm. 2. Continuum validity requires characteristic dimension L ≫ 68 nm. 3. At 10⁻³ mm Hg, mean free path becomes > 1 m. 4. A 1-cm pipe is now smaller than the mean free path → molecules move individually → continuum breaks down. 5. So: continuum needs sufficiently high pressure (or large scale).

✏️ Book Practice

📖 Bansal Ch.1 — Q.1.1 (Conceptual, 2 marks)

What is the continuum concept? Why is it necessary in fluid mechanics?

✅ Answer

Continuum concept assumes fluid properties (density, viscosity, etc.) vary continuously from point to point, ignoring molecular discontinuity. It is necessary because:

  1. It allows use of differential calculus in fluid mechanics.
  2. It simplifies analysis of macroscopic behaviour without tracking individual molecules.
  3. Valid when system size ≫ mean free path (~10⁻⁷ m for air at STP).

2. Fluid Properties

⏱ 90 min 📄 Bansal Ch.1 §1.4–1.9 🏆 3–5 marks

📝 Density, Specific Weight, Specific Gravity

PropertySymbolSI UnitFormula
Mass densityρkg/m³ρ = m/V
Specific weightγN/m³γ = ρg
Specific gravitySdimensionlessS = ρ/ρ_water (ρ_water = 1000 kg/m³)

Newton's Law of Viscosity CRITICAL

τ = μ (du/dy)
  • τ = shear stress (N/m²) · μ = dynamic viscosity (Pa·s) · du/dy = velocity gradient (s⁻¹)
ν = μ / ρ (kinematic viscosity, m²/s)
Must remember: Liquids: viscosity decreases with temperature ↑ (weaker molecular bonding). Gases: viscosity increases with temperature ↑ (molecular momentum transfer ↑).

Surface Tension & Capillarity

h = 4σ cosθ / (ρ g d)
  • σ = surface tension (N/m) · θ = contact angle (water-glass ≈ 0°, mercury-glass ≈ 130°)
  • Water rises in glass; mercury is depressed (negative h).

Vapor Pressure & Cavitation

  • Vapor pressure: pressure at which liquid starts boiling at a given temperature.
  • Cavitation: vapor bubbles form where local pressure < vapor pressure; their violent collapse damages surfaces.

📘 Worked Examples

Plate-sliding viscosity problem (the classic PYQ) — plate at 0.025 mm from a fixed plate, moving at 0.6 m/s, shear 2 N/m².

τ = μ (du/dy) → μ = τ·t / V μ = 2 × (0.025 × 10⁻³) / 0.6 = 8.33 × 10⁻⁵ Pa·s

Capillary rise comparison — 4 mm glass tube, water vs mercury (σ = 0.0725 / 0.52 N/m, θ = 0° / 130°, SG_Hg = 13.6):

h_water = 4×0.0725×cos0° / (1000×9.81×0.004) = 7.39 mm ↑ h_Hg = 4×0.52×cos130° / (13600×9.81×0.004) = −3.09 mm ↓

✏️ Book Practice

📖 Bansal Ch.1 — Q.1.16 (3 marks)

Find kinematic viscosity if dynamic viscosity is 0.08 Pa·s and density 850 kg/m³.

✅ Solution
ν = μ/ρ = 0.08/850 = 9.41 × 10⁻⁵ m²/s
📖 Bansal Ch.1 — Q.1.21 (3 marks)

One litre of liquid weighs 7 N. Find specific weight, density and specific gravity. (Asked verbatim in MO2024 P1)

✅ Solution
γ = W/V = 7 / 10⁻³ = 7000 N/m³ ρ = γ/g = 7000/9.81 = 713.6 kg/m³ S = ρ/1000 = 0.7136

3. Pressure & Pascal's Law

⏱ 30 min 📄 Bansal Ch.2 §2.1–2.3 🏆 2–3 marks

📝 Theory Notes

Pascal's Law: Pressure at any point in a static fluid is equal in all directions.

p = γh = ρgh (pressure at depth h below free surface)
UnitSymbolConversion
PascalPa1 Pa = 1 N/m²
Barbar1 bar = 10⁵ Pa
Atmosphereatm1 atm = 101325 Pa = 10.33 m water
mm of HgmmHg1 mmHg = 133 Pa
Exam tip: "State and prove Pascal's law" is a full 5-mark question in END sem (MO2023 END Q.1(a)). Derive it from the equilibrium of a small fluid element under pressure + body forces.

📘 Worked Example

Pressure at 8 m depth in water; express in bar, atm, mm Hg.

p = 1000 × 9.81 × 8 = 78,480 Pa = 0.7848 bar = 78480/101325 = 0.774 atm = 78480/133 = 589.9 mmHg

4. Total Pressure on Plane Surfaces

⏱ 30 min 📄 Bansal Ch.2 §2.4 🏆 5 marks

📝 Theory Notes

P = ρ g A h̄
  • P = total pressure force (N) · A = area (m²) · h̄ = depth of centroid (m)
Most common PYQ: vertical rectangular surface with top edge at the free surface — appears for 5 marks repeatedly.

📘 Worked Example

Vertical rectangular plate 2 m wide × 3 m deep, top edge at free surface (MO2025 MID Q.2(b)).

A = 2×3 = 6 m² ; h̄ = 3/2 = 1.5 m P = 1000 × 9.81 × 6 × 1.5 = 88,290 N = 88.29 kN

✅ Solution
A = 12 m² ; h̄ = 1 + 4/2 = 3 m P = 1000 × 9.81 × 12 × 3 = 353,160 N = 353.16 kN

5. Center of Pressure

⏱ 45 min 📄 Bansal Ch.2 §2.5 🏆 5–7 marks

📝 Theory Notes

h* = h̄ + I_G / (A · h̄)
  • h* = depth of center of pressure · I_G = moment of inertia about centroidal axis · A = area · h̄ = centroid depth
ShapeI_G about horizontal axis through CG
Rectangle (b × d)bd³/12
Circle (dia D)πD⁴/64
Triangle (base b, height h)bh³/36
Key result: for a rectangular plate with top edge at free surface → h* = (2/3) × depth of lower edge.

📘 Worked Examples

Example 1 — Circular plate (MO2025 END Q.1(a)): D = 1.5 m, centre 3.0 m below free surface.

A = π×1.5²/4 = 1.767 m² ; h̄ = 3.0 m I_G = π×1.5⁴/64 = 0.248 m⁴ h* = 3.0 + 0.248/(1.767 × 3.0) = 3.047 m (CP is 4.7 cm below centre)

Example 2 — Rectangle (MO2025 MID Q.2(b)): 2 m wide × 3 m deep, top edge at surface.

A = 6 m² ; h̄ = 1.5 m ; I_G = 2×3³/12 = 4.5 m⁴ h* = 1.5 + 4.5/(6 × 1.5) = 2.0 m (= 2h/3 rule ✓)

✏️ Book Practice

📖 Bansal Ch.2 — Q.2.3 (5 marks)

Circular plate D = 1.2 m, centre 2 m below water surface. Find P and h*.

✅ Solution
A = π×1.2²/4 = 1.131 m² ; P = 1000×9.81×1.131×2 = 22,170 N I_G = π×1.2⁴/64 = 0.1018 m⁴ h* = 2 + 0.1018/(1.131×2) = 2.045 m

6. Buoyancy, Archimedes' Principle & Metacentre

⏱ 45 min 📄 Bansal Ch.2 §2.6–2.7 🏆 5 marks

📝 Theory Notes

Buoyant Force B = ρ_fluid × g × V_displaced
  • Direction: vertically upward through center of buoyancy (B).
Metacentre M vs CGEquilibrium
M above CGStable
M at CGNeutral
M below CGUnstable

Metacentric Height CRITICAL

GM = I_O / V − BG
  • I_O = moment of inertia of water-plane area · V = submerged volume · BG = distance B→CG
For a rectangular barge (length l, width b): I_O = l·b³/12.

📘 Worked Example

Rectangular barge 10 m × 4 m, draft 1.5 m, CG 1.2 m above keel. Find GM and state stability.

V = 10×4×1.5 = 60 m³ ; I_O = 10×4³/12 = 53.33 m⁴ BM = I_O/V = 53.33/60 = 0.889 m B at 0.75 m below waterline → BG = 1.2 − 0.75 = 0.45 m GM = 0.889 − 0.45 = 0.439 m > 0 → Stable

✏️ Book Practice

📖 Bansal Ch.2 — Q.2.12 (5 marks)

Rectangular pontoon 12 m × 6 m floats in seawater (SG 1.025), draft 2 m, CG 1.5 m above keel. Calculate GM.

✅ Solution
V = 144 m³ ; I_O = 12×6³/12 = 216 m⁴ ; BM = 1.5 m B at 1.0 m below waterline → BG = 0.5 m GM = 1.5 − 0.5 = 1.0 m → Stable

7. Pressure Measurement — Manometers

⏱ 60 min 📄 Bansal Ch.2 §2.8–2.12 🏆 5 marks

📝 Theory Notes

Piezometer tube

p = ρ g h
  • Open tube at the point of measurement. Cannot measure vacuum/gas pressure.

U-Tube Manometer CRITICAL

Level-by-level tracing: p_A/γ + y_A + z_A = p_B/γ + y_B + z_B
p_A = p_B + (ρ_m − ρ) g h [heavier manometric fluid]

Differential U-Tube Manometer CRITICAL

Key technique — trace levels from A to B: add pressure going down, subtract going up.

  • Add ρ_m·g·h when descending into manometer fluid.
  • Add ρ·g·h when descending into pipe fluid.

Inverted U-Tube Manometer

  • Used when pressure difference is small; manometric fluid is lighter (usually oil, SG 0.8).
p_A − p_B = (ρ_A − ρ_m) g h_A − (ρ_B − ρ_m) g h_B

Gauge vs Absolute

p_absolute = p_gauge + p_atm p_atm ≈ 10.33 m water = 760 mm Hg = 1.01325 bar

📘 Worked Example

Differential U-tube: pipe A & B carry water, mercury (13600 kg/m³), h = 0.15 m.

p_A − p_B = (ρ_m − ρ) g h = (13600 − 1000) × 9.81 × 0.15 = 18,533 Pa = 18.53 kPa

8. Stream Lines, Flow Types & Continuity

⏱ 30 min 📄 Bansal Ch.1–2 🏆 2–5 marks

📝 Theory Notes

ConceptDefinitionProperty
Stream lineLine tangent to velocity vector at every pointNo flow across
Path lineActual path traced by a fluid particleTime history
Streak lineLine joining all particles that passed through a pointLagrangian view
Steady flow: stream = path = streak line. Unsteady flow: all three differ.

Types of Flow

TypeDefinition
Steady / UnsteadyProperties constant / varying with time at a point
Uniform / Non-uniformSame / different properties at different points, same time
1D / 2D / 3DVariation in 1 / 2 / 3 directions
Laminar / TurbulentSmooth / chaotic; Re < 2000 / Re > 4000
Rotational / IrrotationalVorticity ≠ 0 / = 0

Continuity Equation (differential form)

∂u/∂x + ∂v/∂y + ∂w/∂z = 0 2D: ∂u/∂x + ∂v/∂y = 0
MO2024 P2 Q.2(a): Derive the 2D continuity equation — a repeated 5-mark question.

📘 Worked Example

Check continuity & rotationality: u = x²y, v = y²z, w = −2xyz − yz² (MO2023 MID Q.3(b)).

∂u/∂x = 2xy ; ∂v/∂y = 2yz ; ∂w/∂z = −2xy − 2yz Sum = 0 → continuity satisfied ✓ Vorticity: ω_x = ∂w/∂y − ∂v/∂z = (−2xz − 2yz) − y² = −2xz − 2yz − y² Evaluate at (2,1,3): ω_x = −12 − 6 − 1 = −19 ≠ 0 → rotational

Also classic: u = 2x, v = −2y → ∂u/∂x + ∂v/∂y = 2 − 2 = 0 ✓, ω_z = ∂v/∂x − ∂u/∂y = 0 → irrotational.

9. Euler's Equation & Bernoulli's Theorem

⏱ 60 min 📄 Bansal Ch.5 🏆 3–5 marks 🔗 Prereq: Continuity

📝 Theory Notes

Euler's equation of motion (along a streamline, ideal fluid):

dp/ρ + g dz + v dv = 0 → ∫dp/ρ + gz + v²/2 = const

Assumptions: inviscid (ideal) fluid · steady flow · along a streamline · incompressible.

Integrating for incompressible flow gives Bernoulli's equation:

p/ρg + v²/2g + z = const (total head)
  • p/ρg = pressure head · v²/2g = velocity head · z = datum head
Guaranteed question: "Derive Bernoulli's theorem and state assumptions" — asked in MO2022 END Q.2(b) and MO2025 MID Q.4(b) (from Navier–Stokes for inviscid steady flow).
For a real fluid there is a head-loss term h_L: p₁/ρg + v₁²/2g + z₁ = p₂/ρg + v₂²/2g + z₂ + h_L.

10. Venturimeter, Orifice Meter, Losses & Boundary Layer

⏱ 90 min 📄 Bansal Ch.5–7 🏆 5 marks

📝 Theory Notes

Venturimeter

Q = C_d · a₁a₂ / √(a₁² − a₂²) · √(2gh)
  • a₁ = inlet area, a₂ = throat area, C_d ≈ 0.96–0.98
  • Mounted horizontal or vertical; h from differential manometer.

Orifice meter

Q = C_d · a₀a₁ / √(a₁² − a₀²) · √(2gh) (a₀ = orifice area, C_d ≈ 0.6–0.65)
MO2023 END Q.3(a) & MO2024 P2 Q.3(a): "Prove that discharge through an orifice meter is Q = C_d·a₀a₁/√(a₁²−a₀²)·√(2gh)" — full derivation, 5 marks.

Major & minor losses

Darcy–Weisbach: h_f = 4f·L·v²/(2g·d) [f = friction factor] Also: h_f = f·L·v²/(2g·d) using Fanning f' = 4f convention
  • Major: friction along the pipe length.
  • Minor: entry, exit, sudden enlargement/contraction, bends, fittings: h = k·v²/2g.

Boundary layer

  • Thin region near a surface where viscosity matters; velocity grows from 0 (no-slip) to free-stream U.
  • Separation occurs in an adverse pressure gradient (pressure increasing downstream) when wall shear reaches zero — creates wakes, increases drag.
  • Control: streamlining (aerofoils), suction, blowing, vortex generators, trip wires (boundary-layer control by acceleration).

📘 Worked Example

Horizontal venturimeter, inlet 30 cm, throat 15 cm, mercury U-tube reading h (MO2025 MID Q.5(b)).

a₁ = π×0.3²/4 = 0.0707 m² ; a₂ = π×0.15²/4 = 0.0177 m² h = h_mano × (S_Hg − 1) = h_mano × (13.6 − 1) (water flowing) Q = C_d a₁a₂√(2gh)/√(a₁²−a₂²) With h_mano = 0.2 m: h = 2.52 m, Q ≈ 0.154 m³/s (C_d = 0.98)

11. Hydraulic Machines — Turbines & Pumps

⏱ 90 min 📄 Bansal Ch. 15–17 🏆 5 marks · END sem

📝 Theory Notes

Classification of turbines

  • Impulse turbines (Pelton): all head converted to kinetic energy in nozzle; runner at atmospheric pressure; jet impinges on buckets. Used for high head, low discharge.
  • Reaction turbines (Francis — inward flow; Kaplan — axial): pressure drops gradually in runner; runner fully submerged. Used for medium/low head.

Pelton wheel key relations

Velocity of jet: V₁ = C_v √(2gH) (C_v ≈ 0.97–0.99) Bucket speed: u = 0.45–0.5 V₁ (max efficiency) Power to runner: P = ρ Q (V₁ − u)(1 + k cosβ) u [k ≈ 0.85–0.9, β = bucket angle] Hydraulic efficiency: η_h = 2(V₁−u)(1+cosβ)u / V₁² Maximum η_h when u = V₁/2: η_max = (1+cosβ)/2 ≈ 0.92–0.97

Francis (inward flow reaction) turbine

η_overall = P_shaft / (ρgQH) Hydraulic efficiency η_h = 0.93–0.96 ; discharge Q = π D₁ b₁ V_f1 u = K_u √(2gH), V_f = K_f √(2gH) (given as 0.26√(2gH) etc.)

Governing of turbines

  • Automatic speed control when load changes, keeping runner speed constant.
  • Impulse (Pelton): spear/needle valve adjusts jet cross-section + deflector plate diverts jet.
  • Reaction (Francis): guide vanes rotate to change flow area.

📘 Worked Example

Pelton wheel (MO2022 END Q.4(c)): tangential velocity 20 m/s, net head H, jet diameter d. Power to runner:

Given u = 20 m/s. Find V₁ = C_v√(2gH) (say C_v = 0.98, H = 40 m → V₁ = 27.4 m/s) Q = a·V₁ = (πd²/4)·V₁ P = ρQ(V₁ − u)(1 + k cosβ)u With d = 0.1 m, k = 0.85, β = 165°: P ≈ 1000 × 0.215 × 7.4 × 0.18 × 20 ≈ 5.7 kW

Common Traps

  • Manometers: always add going down, subtract going up.
  • Rectangular plate with top edge at free surface: h* = 2h/3.
  • Metacentre: GM > 0 stable · GM = 0 neutral · GM < 0 unstable.
  • Capillary rise negative sign = depression (mercury).
  • Absolute = gauge + atmospheric (10.33 m water).
  • Liquids μ ↓ with T ↑; gases μ ↑ with T ↑.
  • Inverted U-tube → light manometric fluid; small Δp.
  • Orifice meter C_d ≈ 0.6, Venturimeter C_d ≈ 0.98.
