1. Continuum Concept (§1.1–1.3)

⏱ 15 min 📄 Bansal Ch.1 §1.1–1.3 🏆 2 marks (MID) 🔗 Prereq: None

📝 Theory Notes

  • Real fluids are molecular, but we treat them as continuum when studying macroscopic behaviour.
  • Assumption valid when characteristic length (e.g. pipe diameter) >> mean free path of molecules.
  • Physical properties (ρ, μ, σ) are treated as continuous, NOT discrete.
Exam tip: Always appear as 2-mark L1 question in MID semesters. Can be asked: "Discuss the concept of continuum with justification" or "What does continuum signify?"

📘 Worked Example (Bansal §1.1–1.3)

Illustrative Problem 1.1 (adapted): Explain why continuum assumption fails at very low pressures (high vacuum).

Solution steps: 1. At standard atmospheric pressure, air molecule mean free path ≈ 68 nm. 2. For continuum validity: characteristic dimension L >> 68 nm. 3. If pressure drops to 10⁻³ mm Hg (high vacuum), mean free path becomes > 1 m. 4. At this scale, a 1-cm diameter pipe is smaller than mean free path → molecules move individually → continuum breaks down. 5. Therefore, continuum assumption requires pressure to be sufficiently high (or scale sufficiently large).

✏️ Book Practice Questions

📖 Bansal Ch.1 — Q.1.1 (Conceptual, 2 marks)

What is the continuum concept? Why is it necessary in fluid mechanics?

✅ Answer

Continuum concept assumes fluid properties (density, viscosity, etc.) vary continuously from point to point, ignoring molecular discontinuity. It is necessary because:

  1. It allows use of differential calculus in fluid mechanics.
  2. It simplifies analysis of macroscopic behaviour without tracking individual molecules.
  3. It is valid when the system size is much larger than mean free path (~10⁻⁷ m for air at STP).

2. Fluid Properties (§1.4–1.6, §1.7–1.8, §1.9)

⏱ 90 min 📄 Bansal Ch.1 §1.4–1.9 🏆 3–5 marks 🔗 Prereq: None

📝 Theory Notes

Density, Specific Weight, Specific Gravity

PropertySymbolSI UnitFormula
Mass densityρkg/m³ρ = m/V
Specific weightγN/m³γ = ρg
Specific gravitySdimensionlessS = ρ/ρ_water (ρ_water = 1000 kg/m³)

Newton's Law of Viscosity CRITICAL

τ = μ (du/dy)
  • τ = shear stress (N/m²)
  • μ = dynamic viscosity (N·s/m² or Pa·s)
  • du/dy = velocity gradient (rate of strain, s⁻¹)

Kinematic viscosity:

ν = μ / ρ   (units: m²/s)
Must remember: Liquids: viscosity decreases with temperature ↑ (molecular bonding weaker). Gases: viscosity increases with temperature ↑ (molecular momentum transfer ↑).

Surface Tension & Capillarity

h = 4σ cosθ / (ρ g d)
  • σ = surface tension (N/m)
  • θ = angle of contact (water-glass ≈ 0°, mercury-glass ≈ 130°)
  • d = tube diameter (m)
  • Water rises in glass; mercury is depressed.

Vapor Pressure & Cavitation

  • Vapor pressure: pressure at which liquid starts boiling at given temperature.
  • Cavitation: Formation of vapor bubbles in regions where local pressure < vapor pressure. Bubbles collapse violently → damage to surfaces.

📘 Worked Example (Bansal §1.5)

Problem: A plate of thickness 0.05 m moves at 0.8 m/s over a fixed plate with 0.01 m gap. Force per unit area required is 3 N/m². Find dynamic viscosity μ.

Given: t = 0.05 m, V = 0.8 m/s, τ = 3 N/m² Formula: μ = τ × (t / V) μ = 3 × (0.05 / 0.8) = 3 × 0.0625 = 0.1875 N·s/m² μ ≈ 0.188 Pa·s

✏️ Book Practice Questions

📖 Bansal Ch.1 — Q.1.16 (Viscosity, 3 marks)

Find the kinematic viscosity of a liquid if its dynamic viscosity is 0.08 Pa·s and density is 850 kg/m³.

✅ Solution
ν = μ / ρ = 0.08 / 850 = 9.41 × 10⁻⁵ m²/s
📖 Bansal Ch.1 — Q.1.21 (Capillary rise, 3 marks)

Compare capillary rise in a 4 mm glass tube for water (σ=0.0725 N/m, θ≈0°) and mercury (σ=0.52 N/m, θ=130°, SG=13.6).

✅ Solution
h_water = 4σ cosθ / (ρ g d) = 4×0.0725×1 / (1000×9.81×0.004) = 0.0147 m = 14.7 mm
h_mercury = 4×0.52×cos130° / (13600×9.81×0.004) = -3.09 mm (depression)

3. Pressure & Pascal's Law (§2.1–2.3)

⏱ 30 min 📄 Bansal Ch.2 §2.1–2.3 🏆 2–3 marks 🔗 Prereq: Fluid Properties

📝 Theory Notes

Pascal's Law: Pressure at any point in a static fluid is equal in all directions.

p = γh = ρgh (pressure at depth h below free surface)

Pressure Units

UnitSymbolConversion
PascalPa1 Pa = 1 N/m²
Barbar1 bar = 10⁵ Pa
Atmosphereatm1 atm = 101325 Pa = 10.33 m water
mm of HgmmHg1 mmHg = 133 Pa

📘 Worked Example (Bansal §2.2)

Problem: Find pressure at 8 m depth in water (ρ = 1000 kg/m³). Also express in bar and atm.

p = ρgh = 1000 × 9.81 × 8 = 78,480 Pa = 0.7848 bar In atm: 78480 / 101325 = 0.774 atm In mm Hg: 78480 / 133 = 589.9 mmHg

✏️ Book Practice Questions

📖 Bansal Ch.2 — Q.2.1 (Pressure, 2 marks)

Define pressure head. A tank contains water to a depth of 5 m. Find pressure at bottom in kPa.

✅ Solution
p = ρgh = 1000 × 9.81 × 5 = 49,050 Pa = 49.05 kPa

4. Total Pressure on Plane Surfaces (§2.4)

⏱ 30 min 📄 Bansal Ch.2 §2.4 🏆 5 marks 🔗 Prereq: Pressure & Pascal's Law

📝 Theory Notes

P = ρ g A h̄
  • P = total pressure force (N)
  • A = area of surface (m²)
  • h̄ = depth of centroid from free surface (m)
Most common PYQ: Vertical rectangular surface with top edge at free surface. Always appears for 5 marks.

📘 Worked Example (Bansal §2.4)

Problem: A vertical rectangular plate 2 m wide × 3 m deep has its top edge at free surface of water. Find total pressure and its position.

Area A = 2 × 3 = 6 m² h̄ = 3/2 = 1.5 m (centroid at mid-depth) P = ρgAh̄ = 1000 × 9.81 × 6 × 1.5 = 88,290 N = 88.29 kN

✏️ Book Practice Questions

📖 Bansal Ch.2 — Q.2.1 (Total pressure, 5 marks)

Find total pressure on a vertical rectangular surface 3 m wide × 4 m deep, top edge 1 m below free surface. Water on one side.

✅ Solution
A = 3 × 4 = 12 m² h̄ = 1 + (4/2) = 3 m P = 1000 × 9.81 × 12 × 3 = 353,160 N = 353.16 kN

5. Center of Pressure (§2.5)

⏱ 45 min 📄 Bansal Ch.2 §2.5 🏆 5–7 marks 🔗 Prereq: Total Pressure

📝 Theory Notes

h* = h̄ + I_G / (A × h̄)
  • h* = depth of center of pressure from free surface
  • I_G = moment of inertia of area about centroidal axis
  • A = total area
  • h̄ = centroid depth

Key shapes:

ShapeI_G about horizontal axis through CG
Rectangle (b × d)bd³/12
Circle (dia D)πD⁴/64
Triangle (base b, height h)bh³/36
Key result: For a rectangular plate with top edge at free surface:
h* = (2/3) × depth_of_lower_edge

📘 Worked Examples

Example 1 — Circular Plate (MO2025 END Q1(a))

Given: D = 1.5 m, center at 3.0 m below free surface, water. Area A = πD²/4 = 1.767 m² h̄ = 3.0 m I_G = πD⁴/64 = 0.248 m⁴ h* = 3.0 + 0.248 / (1.767 × 3.0) = 3.047 m CP is 4.7 cm below center of plate.

Example 2 — Rectangular Surface (MO2025 MID Q2(b))

Given: 2 m wide × 3 m deep, top edge at free surface. A = 2 × 3 = 6 m², h̄ = 1.5 m I_G = bd³/12 = 4.5 m⁴ h* = 1.5 + 4.5 / (6 × 1.5) = 2.0 m (CP at bottom edge — matches h* = 2h/3 rule)

✏️ Book Practice Questions

📖 Bansal Ch.2 — Q.2.3 (Circular plate CP, 5 marks)

Find total pressure and center of pressure on a circular plate of diameter 1.2 m with its center 2 m below water surface.

✅ Solution
A = π×1.2²/4 = 1.131 m², h̄ = 2 m P = 1000×9.81×1.131×2 = 22,170 N I_G = π×1.2⁴/64 = 0.1018 m⁴ h* = 2 + 0.1018/(1.131×2) = 2.045 m

6. Buoyancy, Archimedes' Principle & Metacentre (§2.6–2.7)

⏱ 45 min 📄 Bansal Ch.2 §2.6–2.7 🏆 5 marks 🔗 Prereq: Total Pressure

📝 Theory Notes

Buoyant Force B = ρ_fluid × g × V_displaced
  • Direction: Vertically upward through center of buoyancy (B).
  • Equilibrium conditions:
ConditionMeaning
M above CGStable equilibrium
M at CGNeutral equilibrium
M below CGUnstable equilibrium

Metacentric Height CRITICAL

GM = I_O / V − BG
  • I_O = moment of inertia of water-plane area about axis
  • V = submerged volume
  • BG = distance between center of buoyancy and CG of body
For rectangular barge (length l, width b): I_O = l × b³ / 12

📘 Worked Example (Bansal §2.6)

Problem: A rectangular barge 10 m × 4 m floats in water. Draft is 1.5 m. CG is 1.2 m above keel. Find GM and state stability.

Submerged volume V = 10 × 4 × 1.5 = 60 m³ I_O = l×b³/12 = 10×4³/12 = 53.33 m⁴ BM = I_O / V = 53.33 / 60 = 0.889 m BG = distance between B and CG Center of buoyancy B is at centroid of submerged volume = 0.75 m below waterline. BG = 1.2 − 0.75 = 0.45 m (CG above B) GM = 0.889 − 0.45 = 0.439 m > 0 → Stable

✏️ Book Practice Questions

📖 Bansal Ch.2 — Q.2.12 (Metacentric height, 5 marks)

A rectangular pontoon 12 m × 6 m floats in seawater (SG=1.025) with draft 2 m. If CG is 1.5 m above keel, calculate GM.

✅ Solution
V = 12×6×2 = 144 m³ I_O = 12×6³/12 = 216 m⁴ BM = 216/144 = 1.5 m B is at 1.0 m below waterline. BG = 1.5 − 1.0 = 0.5 m GM = 1.5 − 0.5 = 1.0 m → Stable

7. Pressure Measurement (§2.8–2.12)

⏱ 60 min 📄 Bansal Ch.2 §2.8–2.12 🏆 5 marks 🔗 Prereq: Pressure & Pascal's Law

📝 Theory Notes

Piezometer Tube

p = ρ g h
  • Open tube connected to point where pressure is measured.
  • Cannot measure negative (vacuum) pressure or gas pressure.

U-Tube Manometer CRITICAL

Level-by-level tracing: p_A/γ + y_A + z_A = p_B/γ + y_B + z_B

Simplified forms:

p_A = p_B + (ρ_m − ρ) g h   [when μ-fluid is heavier]
p_A = p_B − (ρ − ρ_m) g h   [when μ-fluid is lighter]

Differential U-Tube Manometer CRITICAL

Problem pattern: Two pipes A and B at different elevations. Manometer fluid: mercury (ρ_m = 13600 kg/m³) or oil.

Key technique: Trace levels from A to B through manometer.

  • Add pressure when going down, subtract when going up.
  • Add ρ_m·g·h when descending INTO manometer fluid.
  • Add ρ·g·h when descending into fluid in pipe.

Inverted U-Tube Manometer

  • Used when pressure difference is small.
  • Manometer fluid is lighter than pipe fluids (usually oil, SG=0.8).
p_A − p_B = (ρ_A − ρ_m) g h_A − (ρ_B − ρ_m) g h_B

GAUGE vs ABSOLUTE Pressure

p_absolute = p_gauge + p_atmospheric

📘 Worked Example (Bansal §2.9)

Problem: Pipe A (water, ρ=1000) and Pipe B (water) connected by differential U-tube with mercury (ρ_m=13600). Height difference h=0.15 m. Find pressure difference.

p_A − p_B = (ρ_m − ρ) g h = (13600 − 1000) × 9.81 × 0.15 = 12600 × 9.81 × 0.15 = 18,533 Pa = 18.53 kPa

✏️ Book Practice Questions

📖 Bansal Ch.2 — Q.2.15 (U-tube manometer, 5 marks)

Pressure in pipe A (water) is measured with mercury manometer. Height difference = 200 mm. Find pressure in A.

✅ Solution
p_A = (ρ_m − ρ) g h = (13600−1000)×9.81×0.2 = 24,711 Pa = 24.71 kPa
📖 Bansal Ch.2 — Q.2.19 (Inverted U-tube, 5 marks)

Inverted U-tube manometer with oil (SG=0.8) connects two water pipes. h = 0.12 m. Find pressure difference.

✅ Solution
p_A − p_B = (ρ_A − ρ_m)g h_A − (ρ_B − ρ_m)g h_B For same fluid in both pipes and symmetric manometer: Δp = (1000 − 800)×9.81×0.12 = 235 Pa

8. Stream Lines, Flow Types & Continuity

⏱ 30 min 📄 Bansal Ch.2 §2.8 + intro 🏆 2–5 marks 🔗 Prereq: None

📝 Theory Notes

ConceptDefinitionProperty
Stream lineLine tangent to velocity vector at every pointNo flow across
Path lineActual path traced by a fluid particleTime history
Streak lineLine joining all particles passed through a pointLagrangian view
Steady flow: stream line = path line = streak line. Unsteady flow: all three are different.

Types of Flow

TypeDefinition
Steady / UnsteadyProperties constant / varying with time at a point
Uniform / Non-uniformProperties same / different at different points at same time
1D / 2D / 3DVariation in 1 / 2 / 3 directions
Laminar / TurbulentSmooth / chaotic; Re < 2000 / Re > 4000
Rotational / IrrotationalVorticity ≠ 0 / = 0 (curl V = 0)

Continuity Equation (Differential Form)

∂u/∂x + ∂v/∂y + ∂w/∂z = 0 For 2D (z=0): ∂u/∂x + ∂v/∂y = 0
MO2022 MID Q3(a): Derive for 2D Cartesian — 2 marks every MID.

📘 Worked Example (Bansal §1.9)

Problem: 2D flow has u = 2x, v = −2y. Check continuity and rotationality.

∂u/∂x = 2, ∂v/∂y = −2 ∂u/∂x + ∂v/∂y = 2 + (−2) = 0 → Continuity satisfied. Vorticity ω_z = ∂v/∂x − ∂u/∂y = 0 − 0 = 0 → Irrotational.

✏️ Book Practice Questions

📖 Bansal Ch.1 — Q.1.25 (Types of flow, 2 marks)

Distinguish between steady and unsteady flow with examples.

✅ Answer

Steady flow: properties do not change with time at any point. Example: flow in a pipe with constant inlet conditions.

Unsteady flow: properties vary with time. Example: flow in a pipe with varying inlet velocity, tidal flows.

⚡ Quick Revision Cheat Sheet — Fluid Statics Module 1

Must-Know Formulas

P = ρ g A h̄
h* = h̄ + I_G/(A·h̄)
B = ρ_fluid × g × V
GM = I_O/V − BG
τ = μ (du/dy)
h = 4σ cosθ / (ρ g d)
Re = ρVD/μ = VD/ν
∂u/∂x + ∂v/∂y = 0

Common Traps

  • In manometer problems: always add when going down, subtract when going up.
  • For rectangular plate with top edge at free surface: h* = 2h/3.
  • Metacentre: GM > 0 → stable; GM = 0 → neutral; GM < 0 → unstable.
  • Capillary rise: negative sign means depression (mercury).
  • Absolute pressure = gauge + atmospheric. Don't forget p_atm = 10.33 m water.
